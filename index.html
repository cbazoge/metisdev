About

Why on-device AI, specifically

I've spent 12+ years writing Swift and Objective-C for iOS, including years on apps used by millions of people.

Somewhere in there I ended up doing the parts of iOS work nobody volunteers for: low-level performance passes, accessibility work, the stuff that has to be exactly right because it touches every screen in the app.

That background is why on-device AI is a natural extension of what I already do, not a pivot into a trend. Core ML and Apple's Foundation Models framework aren't really "AI work" the way a cloud API integration is — they're systems work: memory, threading, model loading, and what happens when a model isn't available on an older device. That's the same category of problem I've been solving in iOS for over a decade.

I also run Metis Dev alone. When I say I built exhala end-to-end — design, code, App Store submission, support — by myself, I mean it. There's no team to hand off to. The person who integrates the on-device AI is the same person who ships it and answers for it afterward.

Services

What I work on

On-device AI integration is the main thing. iOS development, SwiftUI migration, and accessibility audits are real services too, for teams who need the underlying app work done well.

01

On-Device AI Integration

I bring Apple's on-device machine learning into your existing app: the Foundation Models framework for language features, Core ML or MLX for custom models — all running on the user's device instead of a server.

  • No per-request API cost. Inference happens on the phone, so your cost doesn't scale with usage the way a cloud LLM API does.
  • Works with no network connection. The feature keeps working on a plane, in a basement, wherever your users actually are.
  • Nothing leaves the phone. No user data or prompts cross the network, which simplifies your privacy story and your App Store review.

This is what I spend most of my time on, and what I most want to hear about.
